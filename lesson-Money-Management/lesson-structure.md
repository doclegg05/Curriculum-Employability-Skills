## How the parts form one lesson

### Decided

Britt decided these on October 27, 2026.

- Jess opens each segment, and the teams keep their own examples. Jess is the fictional SNAP mom of two from Part 2, and she already has a captioned video. Each segment starts with one Jess moment, such as a payday, a bill, or a suspicious text. Activities keep their current characters, for example the five people in Part 6.
- Britt will propose a full unification to the teams, with every activity rebuilt around Jess. Until they agree, do not rewrite team examples around Jess.
- The chapter map below is the working placeholder the team will discuss. The topic map's original single-lesson skeleton is not presented to the team. `lesson-flow.html` is the shareable two-page view of this flow, with the fonts and logo embedded so it opens anywhere. `lesson-flow.pdf` is its print copy for the team.

Eight segments at about 12 minutes each plus about 25 minutes of shared stages is about 2 hours.

- **Rubric.** The draft is `Handouts/Money_Management_Rubric.docx`, with a one-page print copy at `Handouts/Money_Management_Rubric.pdf`. It is built on the old module's Word rubric so the SPOKES layout and logo stay the same. It has five rows, scored Exceeding 10, Meeting 8, Approaching 5, Emerging 2, then doubled to a final score out of 100:
  1. Know and Plan Your Money. Britt chose to score both activities here: the pay stub with the cash or SNAP sort, and the Cushion Challenge save-first plan.
  2. Keep More of Your Money.
  3. Spend and Protect Your Money.
  4. Real-Life Money Challenge.
  5. Independent Application, which is My Money Plan with its next step.

  The rubric's student expectations are the seven draft objectives. Rows 2 and 3 describe activities that Parts 3 & 5 and 8 have not built yet, so revise those rows when the teams deliver.

## Draft objectives

Britt chose this draft on October 27, 2026. It matches Phase 1:

- It uses the Employee Accountability slide stem.
- Each line starts with a verb.
- Lines are grouped under headings, the way Interview Skills groups its objectives.

The headings are the three content chapters. The teacher lesson plan uses the same lines. Pass marks belong in the rubric, not in the objectives.

**By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:**

*Know and plan your money*

1. Read a pay stub to find your take-home pay, and sort costs into what cash pays for and what SNAP pays for. (Part 1)
2. Make a plan to save a little before you spend. (Part 2)

*Keep more of your money*

3. Find small costs and bills that drain your money, and figure out what they cost in a year. (Parts 4 and 6)
4. Track a week of money in and money out, and use two ways to stretch it at the store. (Part 3 & 5)

*Spend and protect your money*

5. Recognize buying pressure and scam warning signs, and know when to walk away. (Parts 8 and 7)
6. Choose a smart response to a real-life money problem. (Part 9)
7. Choose one next step for this week, and know where to ask for help. (Application)
