Warm-Up | listening assessment

Start on a worksheet, not a slide.

Instead of opening with stacked cards, the warm-up becomes a paper-like prompt sheet. The lesson feels more like a guided studio session from the first minute.

Prompt sheet
Rate yourself honestly.

Complete the first column of the Personal Listening Assessment before instruction begins.

Recall one recent conversation.

Were you actually listening, or just waiting for your turn to speak?

Prepare to share a reason.

Choose one rating you would defend in front of the class and explain why.

attention eye contact tone patience feedback
Introduction | default video screen

Watch how effective communication changes the outcome.

This is the default video rule for the redesign: a brief introduction above the screen, then one large video with no competing cards, prompts, or side content on the page.

Presentation 1 | communication cycle

Teach the cycle as a blueprint.

A process slide gives the concept its own visual grammar. It feels different from the warm-up and different from the later workshop slides.

01

Sender

The person who begins the message and chooses the intention.

02

Encode

Words, tone, posture, and format turn the idea into a signal.

03

Channel

Face-to-face, email, phone, or public announcement changes how the message lands.

04

Decode

The receiver interprets what they hear, see, and feel.

05

Respond

Feedback shows whether the meaning matched the intention.

Feedback loop

The message is not finished when it is spoken. It closes when the speaker checks whether the receiver understood it the way it was meant.

Class prompt

Think of a time when your message was decoded differently than you intended. Which part of the cycle broke down: the words, the tone, the channel, or the follow-up?

Presentation 2 | communication styles

Compare the three styles as unequal towers.

Verbal, body, and voice can be taught as a studio comparison instead of tabs. The learner can read the relationships in one glance.

Verbal message

Words

  • Pronunciation and grammar shape credibility.
  • Vocabulary should match the audience and the setting.
  • Slang and swearing weaken professional public communication.
Body message

Presence

  • Facial expression should support the message you want received.
  • Posture, eye contact, and gestures signal confidence and care.
  • People often believe your body before they believe your script.
Voice message

Tone

  • Pitch, volume, and rate affect how quickly meaning is understood.
  • Enunciation helps the public process information under pressure.
  • Voice often reveals emotion even when words are controlled.
Presentation 2 | comparison video wall

Compare how the same topic plays out in different scenarios.

This multi-view screen is a limited exception. Use it only when learners need to compare one communication problem across several related scenarios, not as the standard treatment for video in the curriculum.

Presentation 3 | self-advocacy

Frame advocacy as a map, not a list.

Self-advocacy is the ability to express your needs, make informed choices, and seek support respectfully. A map helps learners see that advocacy is built from several connected capacities.

Know yourself

Recognize strengths, needs, goals, and what support actually helps.

Know your rights

Understand workplace expectations, resources, and protections before you speak up.

Know your ask

State clearly what you need, why it matters, and what next step would help.

Self-Advocacy center of action
Personal

Confidence and self-awareness.

Professional

Asking for clarity, feedback, and opportunity.

Support

Finding mentors, allies, and useful programs.

Action

Speaking clearly, following up, and persisting respectfully.

Structural redesign takeaway

Different pages. Same curriculum.

This pilot asks a simple question: if the lesson looked less like one template repeated and more like a sequence of purposeful pages, would the learning feel more memorable?

Structure changed

Worksheet, ribbon, studio, board, atlas, and form each have a distinct job.

Content stayed real

The concepts still come from the current lesson rather than from invented filler.

Decision question

Does this feel meaningfully more identifiable and more useful than the current system?
