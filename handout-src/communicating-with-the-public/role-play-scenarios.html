Communicating with the Public - Role-Play Scenarios

Purpose: Practice clear, empathetic, and accurate communication using real-world situations.

Instructions:

  • Break into pairs, small groups, or large groups depending on class size.
  • Assign roles: Communicator and Public.
  • After each role-play, discuss:
    • Was the message clear?
    • Was the tone respectful?
    • Were questions answered accurately?
Scenario 1

Announcing a Community Event

Context: Your organization is hosting a free health fair next month.

Communicator Task: Explain event details and encourage participation.

Public Questions:
  • Is it really free?
  • What services will be available?
  • Do I need to register?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 2

Responding to a Complaint

Context: A resident is upset about a recent change in trash collection days.

Communicator Task: Respond calmly, explain the reason for the change, and offer solutions.

Public Questions:
  • Why wasn’t I told?
  • This is inconvenient for me!

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 3

Emergency Alert

Context: A severe storm is approaching, and you need to inform the public about safety measures.

Communicator Task: Deliver a clear, urgent message with instructions.

Public Questions:
  • Where can we go for shelter?
  • How long will the storm last?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 4

Explaining a New Policy

Context: Your organization introduces a new recycling program.

Communicator Task: Explain the policy benefits and steps clearly.

Public Questions:
  • Why is this necessary?
  • Will it cost us more?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 5

Handling Misinformation

Context: A rumor spreads that tap water is unsafe, but it’s not true.

Communicator Task: Correct misinformation calmly and provide facts.

Public Questions:
  • I saw it online—how do I know you’re telling the truth?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 6

Budget Announcement

Context: Your department announces a budget cut that affects community programs.

Communicator Task: Explain the reason for the cut and reassure the public about essential services.

Public Questions:
  • Will my program be canceled?
  • Why are you cutting funding now?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 7

Public Safety Update

Context: A new traffic pattern is being implemented to reduce accidents.

Communicator Task: Explain the change and its benefits.

Public Questions:
  • Will this cause delays?
  • How long will construction last?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 8

Responding to Negative Media Coverage

Context: A local news outlet reports inaccurate information about your organization.

Communicator Task: Address the misinformation professionally and restore trust.

Public Questions:
  • Is the report true?
  • Why didn’t you respond sooner?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 9

Promoting a New Service

Context: Your organization launches a new online payment system for utilities.

Communicator Task: Explain how to use it and its benefits.

Public Questions:
  • Is it secure?
  • What if I don’t have internet access?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Scenario 10

Crisis Communication

Context: A chemical spill occurs near a residential area.

Communicator Task: Inform residents about safety precautions and evacuation plans.

Public Questions:
  • Is my family safe?
  • When can we return home?

Variations: 1-on-1, Small group, Large group

Facilitator Instructions

  • Encourage participants to use plain language, empathy, and active listening.
  • After each scenario, ask:
    • What worked well?
    • What could be improved?
  • Reinforce clarity, respect, and accuracy as key principles.
  • Allow time for feedback and discussion after each role-play.
