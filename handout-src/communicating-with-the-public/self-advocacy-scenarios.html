Self-Advocacy Role-Play Scenarios

Family & Personal Self-Advocacy Scenarios

1

Setting Boundaries with a Family Member In-Person

A relative constantly criticizes your parenting choices. You need to have a respectful conversation to set clear boundaries.

2

Addressing a Conflict with a Teenager In-Person

Your teenager has been staying out late without checking in. You need to express concerns, set expectations, and agree on a solution.

3

Asking a Family Member for Support Phone or In-Person

You need help with childcare, transportation, or finances, but your family member has been reluctant. You need to make a clear and reasonable request.

4

Handling an Unfair Family Obligation In-Person

You are always expected to take care of certain family responsibilities, and it is becoming overwhelming. You need to speak up and request a fairer division of tasks.

5

Advocating for Yourself at a Family Gathering In-Person

A family member makes an inappropriate or hurtful comment about your lifestyle, career, or parenting. You need to stand up for yourself without escalating the situation.

School & Child Advocacy Scenarios

6

Requesting an IEP or 504 Plan for Your Child Email

Your child needs additional academic support due to a learning disability. You need to email the school to formally request an evaluation.

7

Reporting Bullying at School Phone

Your child has been bullied, and previous reports to the school have not led to action. You need to call the principal and demand a concrete response.

8

Disputing an Unfair School Disciplinary Action Email or In-Person

Your child was disciplined for something you believe was unfair. You need to request a meeting to discuss the situation with the school administration.

9

Requesting a Meeting with a Teacher Email or Phone

Your child is struggling in a particular class, and you want to discuss concerns with their teacher. You need to email or call to schedule a meeting.

10

Addressing a Safety Concern at School Phone or In-Person

You recently noticed a safety issue at your child's school (e.g., unsafe playground, lack of supervision). You need to bring it to the administration’s attention.

Job Searching Self-Advocacy Scenarios

11

Requesting a Professional Reference Phone or Email

You are applying for a job and need a professional reference from a former employer. You need to ask them for a strong recommendation.

12

Following Up on a Job Interview Email

You had an interview and haven’t heard back. You need to send a professional follow-up email to check on the hiring process.

13

Negotiating Salary for a Job Offer In-Person or Phone

You received a job offer, but the salary is lower than expected. You need to advocate for a higher salary or better benefits.

14

Asking for More Details About a Job Offer Email or Phone

You received a job offer, but some details (schedule, benefits, responsibilities) are unclear. You need to ask for clarification before accepting.

15

Requesting Feedback After a Job Rejection Email

You applied for a job but didn’t get it. You want to email the hiring manager to ask for constructive feedback to improve in the future.

Workplace Readiness & Self-Advocacy Scenarios

16

Requesting a Workplace Accommodation Phone or Email

You need a reasonable workplace accommodation (e.g., flexible schedule, ergonomic chair, extra training). You need to advocate for your needs professionally.

17

Addressing an Issue with a Supervisor Email or In-Person

You feel you are being treated unfairly at work (e.g., excessive workload, unclear expectations). You need to communicate your concerns with your supervisor.

18

Asking for a Promotion or Raise In-Person

You have been excelling at your job and believe you deserve a promotion or raise. You need to have a professional conversation with your manager.

19

Responding to Unfair Criticism at Work In-Person

A colleague or supervisor has unfairly criticized your work. You need to address the issue calmly and professionally.

20

Reporting Workplace Harassment or Discrimination Phone, Email, or In-Person

You have experienced or witnessed harassment/discrimination at work. You need to report it to HR while protecting yourself.
